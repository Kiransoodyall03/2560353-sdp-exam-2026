1 Service overview
The Mars Habitation Experiment (MHE) is a 90-day isolation mission: a crew of five lives in a sealed,
nine-room habitat that simulates a Mars base. Each crew member wears a vitals monitor, each room
monitors its environment, and the crew fill in wellbeing surveys twice a day. Topic prefix: arl/mhe.

1.1 General state and routines
• Mission clock: the mission runs for 90 days; mission_day counts from 1. The live deployment joins
  the mission part-way through (day 40), so earlier days are only in the ARL API backlog. After day 90
  the surveys stop and mission_state becomes complete.
• Reporting: crew vitals every 60 s, room environment and the room-specific streams every 300 s (5
  min).
• Daily routine: the crew follow a daily routine (local time) of sleep in their own quarters, meals and
  exercise in common, and work blocks in the lab or on hydroponics shifts. Crew location and room
  crew_count show where everyone is.
• Surveys: each crew member fills in a start-of-day survey in the morning and an end-of-day survey in
  the evening. A survey not done by 09:00 (start of day) or 23:30 (end of day) is missed.
• EVAs: about twice a week, two crew members go outside on a weekday afternoon for 2 to 3 hours.
  They enter the airlock, it depressurises (10 min), they go outside, return, and it repressurises (10
  min). EVA start and end are published as events.
• Rooms: each room’s temperature, humidity and ventilation follow its set points, and occupants add
  heat, humidity and CO₂. Sealing a room, disabling its environmental systems or a ventilation failure
  stops the air exchange, so CO₂ builds up in an occupied room. The airlock drops to Mars pressure
  (about 0.7 kPa) when depressurised.
• Lighting: each room has a daily lighting window and level (dim, 5 lx, outside the window). The
  hydroponics bay has strong grow lights.
• Hydroponics: a 400 L reservoir feeds the crops. It is drained by evaporation and the plants and
  refilled by the irrigation schedule. The crop goes through a 30-day cycle (germination, vegetative,
  flowering, harvest) with an estimated yield.
• Load: about 0.1 messages/s (5 vitals a minute, 13 room and habitat streams every 5 minutes).

Telemetry and event payloads carry an envelope: v (schema version, integer, currently 1), ts (simulated
time, Unix milliseconds), seq (per-topic sequence number, restarts with the service; gaps reveal lost
messages) and entity (entity id). The measurement fields are added next to the envelope. A
measurement that cannot be taken is null. State payloads are {"v", "ts", "entity", "item",
"value"}.

Commands are JSON {"command_id": <string>, "researcher": <researcher code>, "value":
<value>}. The researcher must be verified by the ARL API for the service. Acknowledgements carry
command_id, target, control, researcher, state (accepted, rejected, in_progress, completed,
failed), progress (0 to 1 for long-running controls), reason and detail. Rejection and failure reasons:
controls_disabled, malformed, unknown_control, unknown_target, unknown_researcher, researcher_
defunct, researcher_not_permitted, api_unavailable, invalid_value, precondition_failed, busy
(the target is already running a long command), fault, timeout, interrupted.
Alerts are events on arl/mhe/events/alert with alert_id, type (the alert key below), severity (info,
warning, critical), state (raised or cleared), value, limit, unit, message and metadata. An alert
that escalates is raised again with the higher severity under the same alert_id. Limits come from the
ARL API (/v1/alert-limits/ + mhe).

5 Configuration
The service reads a TOML file (config/ + mhe.toml in the bundle, --config or ARL_CONFIG for a binary).
Environment variables override the file, and command-line options override both (see Running the ARL).
Defaults are the values in the provided file.
