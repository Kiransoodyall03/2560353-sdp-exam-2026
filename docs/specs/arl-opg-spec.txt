1 Service overview
The On-demand Power Generation (OPG) service is the ARL’s power plant: a solar array, a battery bank
and diesel backup generators that together supply the whole laboratory. Topic prefix: arl/opg.

1.1 General state and routines
• Reporting: every entity reports once a minute (60 s) on its power stream.
• Load: the lab’s demand follows the time of day and the weather (heating and cooling), the activity of
  the other laboratory services, and load shedding.
• Generation priority: each minute the plant covers the load from its sources in the order set by the
  generation priority (solar_first by default). Surplus solar charges the batteries; anything beyond
  that is curtailed. The batteries are not discharged below the battery reserve except in an emergency.
• Automatic backup: the generators start automatically when the batteries reach the reserve and the
  load is not covered, and stop again once the batteries have recovered and solar covers the load. A
  manual start or stop holds until the next automatic trigger.
• Generators: a start takes 30 s (starting) and can fail. A stop runs the generators unloaded for a 60 s
  cool-down. They burn fuel in proportion to their output.
• Refuelling: a fuel delivery of up to 15,000 L arrives every 7 days at 10:00 local (capped at the tank
  size), published as a refuel event.
• Solar: output follows the sun and the weather, and falls slowly as dust builds up on the panels; rain
  cleans them.
• Load: 4 messages a minute (about 0.07 messages/s), plus state changes, refuels and alerts.

Telemetry and event payloads carry an envelope: v (schema version, integer, currently 1), ts (simulated
time, Unix milliseconds), seq (per-topic sequence number, restarts with the service; gaps reveal lost
messages) and entity (entity id). The measurement fields are added next to the envelope. A
measurement that cannot be taken is null. State payloads are {"v", "ts", "entity", "item",
"value"}.

Commands are JSON {"command_id": <string>, "researcher": <researcher code>, "value":
<value>}. The researcher must be verified by the ARL API for the service. Acknowledgements carry
command_id, target, control, researcher, state (accepted, rejected, in_progress, completed,
failed), progress (0 to 1 for long-running controls), reason and detail. Rejection and failure reasons:
controls_disabled, malformed, unknown_control, unknown_target, unknown_researcher, researcher_
defunct, researcher_not_permitted, api_unavailable, invalid_value, precondition_failed, busy
(the target is already running a long command), fault, timeout, interrupted.
Alerts are events on arl/opg/events/alert with alert_id, type (the alert key below), severity (info,
warning, critical), state (raised or cleared), value, limit, unit, message and metadata. An alert
that escalates is raised again with the higher severity under the same alert_id. Limits come from the
ARL API (/v1/alert-limits/ + opg).

5 Configuration
The service reads a TOML file (config/ + opg.toml in the bundle, --config or ARL_CONFIG for a binary).
Environment variables override the file, and command-line options override both (see Running the ARL).
Defaults are the values in the provided file.
