1 Service overview
The On-demand Water Treatment (OWT) service recycles the laboratory’s water. Used (grey) water
collects in the intake tank, the treatment plant cleans it, and the clean water returns to storage for reuse.
Topic prefix: arl/owt.

 Entity                   Kind              Plant
 global                   service           The plant as a whole: water consumed, power used, recycling efficiency.
 intake                   tank              Greywater collection tank, 20,000 L.
 treatment                plant             Filtration, UV and chemical dosing, 0 to 600 L/h.
 storage                  tank              Clean water storage, 50,000 L.

1.1 General state and routines
• Reporting: the plant is simulated every minute but reports every 1,800 s (30 min) on each entity’s
  water stream. This is the slowest service at the ARL.
• Water cycle: about 60 residents use clean water from storage, with peaks in the morning and the
  evening. Most of it returns to the intake as greywater; the rest is lost. The treatment plant pumps
  greywater from the intake at its cleaning rate (default set point 350 L/h), returns most of it to storage
  as clean water and rejects the rest as brine.
• Diverting the intake: when the intake is diverted, greywater goes to the evaporation pond instead of
  the intake. This protects the plant from contaminated water, but the water is lost.
• Storage reserve: the plant keeps a minimum clean volume in storage. When clean water runs out, the
  borehole supplies the residents instead; that water is consumed but not recycled.
• Filters: filter health falls as water is treated. Dirty filters reduce the cleaning rate and the water
  quality. A backwash (30 min, treatment paused, uses 500 L of clean water) restores much of the filter
  health.
• Water quality: treated water should be clear (low turbidity) and near neutral pH. Running the plant
  hard (above about 450 L/h) or with dirty filters makes the water cloudier.
• Recycling efficiency: clean water returned to storage over water consumed, over the trailing 24 h.
• Load: 4 messages every 30 minutes, plus state changes, backwash events and alerts.

 Topic                                     Retained          Content

Telemetry and event payloads carry an envelope: v (schema version, integer, currently 1), ts (simulated
time, Unix milliseconds), seq (per-topic sequence number, restarts with the service; gaps reveal lost
messages) and entity (entity id). The measurement fields are added next to the envelope. A
measurement that cannot be taken is null. State payloads are {"v", "ts", "entity", "item",
"value"}.

Commands are JSON {"command_id": <string>, "researcher": <researcher code>, "value":
<value>}. The researcher must be verified by the ARL API for the service. Acknowledgements carry
command_id, target, control, researcher, state (accepted, rejected, in_progress, completed,
failed), progress (0 to 1 for long-running controls), reason and detail. Rejection and failure reasons:
controls_disabled, malformed, unknown_control, unknown_target, unknown_researcher, researcher_
defunct, researcher_not_permitted, api_unavailable, invalid_value, precondition_failed, busy
(the target is already running a long command), fault, timeout, interrupted.
Alerts are events on arl/owt/events/alert with alert_id, type (the alert key below), severity (info,
warning, critical), state (raised or cleared), value, limit, unit, message and metadata. An alert
that escalates is raised again with the higher severity under the same alert_id. Limits come from the
ARL API (/v1/alert-limits/ + owt).

5 Configuration
The service reads a TOML file (config/ + owt.toml in the bundle, --config or ARL_CONFIG for a binary).
Environment variables override the file, and command-line options override both (see Running the ARL).
Defaults are the values in the provided file.
